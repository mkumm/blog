When I was many many years under the legal drinking age, my father would let me bring him his beer. Often pre-opened and often shy a few sips. I hear for most people alcohol is acquired taste. From the first taste I loved it! It provided some crisp deliciousness I couldn't get from juice or soda pop. 

This is not an addiction story. For some reason I never got the cravings that other people struggle with. Sugar is my addiction, and I am glad it we are allowed to eat a donut and still drive! Beer has always been a way to have a conversation, share a celebration, or just relax on a nice summer day. It was Budweiser that got me started, fortunately my tastes have matured over the years. 

I remember taking a flight to Copenhagen and as the plane was coming in for a landing and the pilot was sharing the local weather etc, he ended with "...and if this is the first time in our city, let me caution you against drinking the water. It is perfectly safe to drink, but our beer is soooo much better!" The pilot was right and that kicked off my first real taste for European beers, and long before the rise of craft beers in America. It was like a whole new world opened up to me. I saw 0.0 beer served in offices and the cultural faux pas of suggesting someone have a beer who has to drive home. 

As the popularity of craft beers started to rise in the US, I got to explore an even wider variety of beers from some great regional breweries like [Griffen Claw](https://griffinclawbrewingcompany.com/), [Detroit Beer Co](https://detroitbeerco.com/), and [Elephant & Co.](https://www.elephantand.com/) and countless others. If it was new me - I wanted it. each new place I traveled was an opportunity to try a new variety of beers. 

For 2 years, probably 90% of the beers I drank were from Belgium.
